# What Crop Finder can assess

Release 2026-11-09.global.3 provides 107 searchable crops across 25 botanical families with 295 reviewed general environmental traits, 96 FAO-56 four-stage growth records across 24 crops, and 18 reviewed pest/disease context records across 15 crops. Release 2026-11-08.global.2 originally added 32 searchable crops (14 with general traits). Of the five crops covered by the Sri Lankan rotation pack, all five also have general references. The catalogue records the highest level available anywhere. The result card records the level actually used for the selected farm.

| Question | Evidence in this release | Meaning for the farmer |
|---|---|---|
| Do the dates usually have temperatures this crop prefers? | Nine crops have an optimum range of **daily mean air temperature** for growth. | The card compares typical daily weather during the farmer's dates with that range. A temperature outside the optimum can still permit growth. |
| Does my measured pH fit? | Cassava, potato and sweet potato have a cited general preference. Some crops have references in the Sri Lankan pack. | A farmer's measured value can be compared with a reference. Missing pH stays unknown. Laboratory methods and variation within a field can affect the comparison. |
| Will the crop get enough water? | Twelve crops have paired indicative full-cycle water-use and duration ranges. | The general screen compares assumed supply with approximate crop water use only when the dates cover a representative full cycle and the farmer supplies enough information. |
| Can I plant again in the next season? | Five reviewed crops have two-season comparisons in a farmer-confirmed Sri Lankan lowland dry/intermediate setting. | The existing rotation engine checks hard constraints first. A general crop card supplies no rotation evidence or yield forecast. |

## Calculations and ordering

Crop Finder verifies that the NASA dataset belongs to the selected coordinates, and that the dataset and daily observations identify a live or verified cached source. An explicit date window is required. The general screen counts each complete historical year once. It takes the median daily temperature within each recurring window, then the median of those yearly medians. It also reports the median proportion of days within the published range across valid years. Those proportions describe past **weather**. At least ten windows with every daily mean present are required. A window crossing New Year is evaluated as one season. The field pH comparison remains available with shorter weather histories.

For the water comparison, years need complete precipitation coverage for the chosen dates. The crop-use (ET) range applies to a full growing cycle. Compare supply only when the entered dates fall within the cited duration range, at least ten seasonal rain totals exist, and retained rainfall fraction and net irrigation are known. A farmer explicitly choosing rainfed supplies a zero irrigation amount. A qualitative description of irrigation access by itself leaves the amount unknown. Estimated supply in mm equals median window rain × farmer-selected usable fraction + net irrigation. Soil water reserves, timing, runoff, local crop variety and irrigation efficiency can change water availability. FAO's rough ET values describe favourable conditions. Paddy preparation, seepage and ponding need additional information.

Persistent waterlogging blocks field tomato in the general screen because the reference documents this conflict. For other global crops a report of persistent standing water calls for checking drainage locally unless a reviewed constraint applies. A short season warns the farmer to check cultivar duration. Indicative days to harvest vary too widely to exclude every cultivar with that warning.

A general crop with only a listed water range can appear with **More information needed**. Crops with no applicable reviewed trait remain searchable in the catalogue. A missing number contributes no favourable evidence. Results group by assessment status, then by locally applicable support, then alphabetically. Items within a group have no scientific rank. A crop with a favourable temperature result can still be unsuitable because of unassessed soil, water, cultivar, pests or planting dates.

In regional mode, the finder uses the first slot of the existing two-season assessment to display crop conditions. A regional block applies to that crop throughout the results. The full sequence report retains second-season feasibility and preference intervals. A general reference for the same crop cannot replace a regional constraint. Global crops, including perennials, have no inferred rotation or nitrogen credits.

## Numerical references

- Eight general temperature intervals come from the optimum column in Table 2 of the MSU CRIES manual, which attributes the data to FAO (1979). The selected facts cover cabbage, groundnut, maize, onion, sorghum, soybean, sunflower and tomato. The original table distinguishes daily mean temperatures from extremes. Generic bean/millet values and ambiguous entries were excluded. The maize interval belongs to this general source. The Sri Lankan pack still lacks a local maize temperature range. [Schultink, Amaral and Mokma, 1987, pp. 10–11](https://www.canr.msu.edu/csus/uploads/files/schultin/CRIES_YIELD_MODEL.pdf)
- FAO Paper 66 supplies the spring/winter wheat temperature reference and the field tomato waterlogging constraint. Vernalisation, changes in day length and crop stage can affect the outcome. [Steduto et al., 2012, wheat and tomato chapters](https://www.fao.org/4/i2800e/i2800e.pdf)
- Indicative ET and duration come from Tables 14 and 6. The maize duration refers to grain maize and the onion duration to dry bulb onion. Any local cycle information should be checked before using this comparison. [Brouwer and Heibloem, FAO Training Manual 3, chapter 3](https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e07.htm)
- The root and tuber pH ranges are from Table 1, attributed to Kay (1973) via Horton (1988). They have no specified soil pH test method. The report also distinguishes perennial and annual growth habits. [CGIAR Technical Advisory Committee/FAO, Appendix 4](https://www.fao.org/4/x5791e/x5791e0q.htm)

Numerical ranges are selected factual observations or indicative references. Each entry retains its original unit, source, scope, retrieval date, release and review status in the knowledge store. The source publications retain their original rights. Their tables, diagrams, text and software are not distributed in the seed.

## Names, classification and attribution

On 8 November 2026 the live AGROVOC Skosmos search and label endpoints returned a reviewed concept and three preferred labels (English, French and Spanish) for each of the 32 crops. Some terms identify commodities such as grain or oilseed. They enable search. They do not identify a particular cultivar or confer environmental suitability. The release also includes curated English search aliases, such as *peanut* for groundnut. Canola is a narrower search alias for rapeseed. The scope of a name can differ across varieties. ## Regional evidence

The extensible directory in `data/regional_evidence.json` identifies country-level references. It gives the Sri Lankan pack its documented scope. It links India to ICAR district contingency plans, Kenya to KALRO crop guidance, Brazil to MAPA's ZARC zoning, the US to USDA state planting and harvest dates, and Australia to NSW sowing resources. Those five countries receive general screening. Their local rules await crop, location, cultivar, season, version and reuse review. These directory links do not activate any unreviewed numerical rules.

## Verification

The screening regression tests use six real archived POWER locations, one in each demonstration country. They exercise regional blocks, pH, water, partial coverage, coordinate and simulation rejection, source records and multilingual names. They verify behaviour against the stated rules. No independent crop-outcome trial has validated the cards. A new numerical or cultivar-specific trait needs provenance, unit, applicability and conflict review before use. A change in the climate metric or support level requires a matching scientific test.
